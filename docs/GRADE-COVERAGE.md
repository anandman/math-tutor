# Math practice coverage, grades 1–4

Updated November 20, 2026. Bakery Quest offers eight pathways with 145 selected
skills, alongside its arithmetic tracks. The grade labels organize practice and
can be changed freely. This is broad practice coverage, not a complete curriculum
checklist or a mapping to a child's STAR domain score. A teacher's sequence can
guide which skills to try first. The report shows only activities actually used.

## What is available

| Grade | Number sense, operations and stories | Fractions, geometry, measurement and data |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Counting through 120; tens/ones, comparison and ten more/less; equality including reversed equations; three-addend sums and making ten; joining, separating, part/whole and comparison stories with varied unknowns. | Hours/half hours; building halves/quarters; shape names, composition and construction; compare/order/estimate lengths and measure with different units; read/build picture charts and find totals. |
| **2** | Hundreds/tens/ones, expanded form, skip counting and comparisons within 1,000; model unit exchanges and regrouping; odd/even pairs and arrays; two-step stories, comparisons, matching equations and repeated-addition stories. | Five-minute clocks, a.m./p.m., U.S. coins/bills; thirds, equal-area shares and share sizes; sides/corners, solids, polygon construction and different shapes with equal area; rulers including nonzero starts, length number lines and units; bar charts, combined comparisons and whole-unit plot construction. |
| **3** | Add/subtract within 1,000, rounding to tens/hundreds, multiply by tens; unknown factors/divisors and partial products; both meanings of division, two-step stories and choosing a calculation plan. | Clocks to the minute, elapsed time and finishing times; fraction number lines, unit-fraction steps, visual equivalence and same-whole comparisons; overlapping quadrilateral families and construction; tiled area, perimeter, grams/liters and equal-perimeter rectangles with different areas; scaled graphs, fractional plots and two-step data questions. |
| **4** | Place value through 1,000,000, ten-times relationships, rounding and comparisons; larger addition/subtraction, four-digit × one-digit and two-digit × two-digit products, exact quotients and remainders; factors/multiples, primes/composites and patterns; additive versus multiplicative comparisons, multistep plans and interpreting leftovers. | Further fraction equivalence, like-denominator mixed-number addition/subtraction, fraction × whole-number models, tenths/hundredths and decimal comparisons; parallel/perpendicular lines, angles, symmetry construction and right triangles; unit/time conversions, money/change stories, missing angles, protractor construction, missing sides/combined areas and area/perimeter designs; eighth-unit plots, fractional totals/differences and comparing two plots. |

Addition/subtraction fact tracks separately offer missing result, missing second
number, and missing first number within 10, 20, and 100. Each form/range has its
own unlocks and reporting. These ranges describe difficulty, not grade placement.
Existing multiplication/division and fraction/decimal practice remains available.

## How to use the pathways

Choose **Math Pathways → grade → pathway → skill**. Every skill provides an
example, guided activity, and independent practice. Grade changes are always
available, including direct entry into grade 4. Eight independent answers in a
ten-question round suggest a next step; support and timeouts remain separate.

For a second grader working on measurement/data and geometry, useful entry points
include **Time & Money**, ruler/number-line tasks in **Measurement**, combined
category comparisons and plot building in **Data & Graphs**, and construction or
equal-area activities in **Shapes & Space**. These are choices to try, not a
diagnosis based on broad domain scores.

**Bakery Challenges** mix grade-appropriate tasks across pathways: open the
bakery, prepare orders, or design the bakery. Their report entries are separate
from focused practice. They provide mixed application practice; they do not
establish transfer to unfamiliar classroom or real-world tasks by themselves.

The optional starting check samples only two activities per grade. A suggestion
is a place to begin, not a grade-level assessment. Guided examples and starting
checks are not scored. Guided completion is visible in the report without being
counted as independent performance.

## What a practice report can and cannot show

The app checks generated answers, selections and constructions. It varies story
roles, diagrams, numbers and models, and accepts alternate valid constructions
where the task allows them. It does not evaluate a child's spoken explanation,
handwritten work, physical ruler use, or performance across a complete school
curriculum. Each skill has a finite set of generated variants; repeated practice
is not an independent standardized assessment.

Time limits are optional and configurable. A timeout is recorded separately from
an incorrect answer. In-app hints, charts and retries mark an answer as supported;
the app cannot detect help given outside it or determine why a child needs time.
No untried skills or non-math subjects are assigned scores.

Advanced fraction/decimal multiplication and division remain optional extensions.
They are not all fourth-grade requirements: grade 4 emphasizes fraction ×
whole-number work, like-denominator fraction arithmetic, and decimal
notation/comparison to hundredths.
