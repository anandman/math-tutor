# Math coverage and next checks, grades 1–4

Reviewed November 20, 2026. This compares Bakery Quest with selected Common Core
expectations to guide future practice. It is not a complete curriculum checklist
or a mapping to a child's STAR score. A teacher's sequence can guide which skills
to try first. The report card continues to show only actual practice.

The missing-number update adds three forms to both addition and subtraction:
missing result, missing second number, and missing first number. Each has its own
practice within 10, 20, and 100. These ranges describe difficulty, not a child's
grade placement. The bakery stories cover adding to and taking from a quantity;
other story structures and multistep reasoning still need separate practice.

## Recommended next additions

For a second grader working on measurement/data and geometry, prioritize **time
and money**, **length estimation and comparison**, and **graph questions that
combine or compare categories**. Follow with shape construction and different
ways to make equal shares. These extend existing visual pathways instead of
assuming that recognizing one familiar diagram demonstrates the entire domain.

Across grades, a **place-value pathway** and **varied word-problem pathway** would
fill large gaps. For a fourth grader, larger-number arithmetic and visual
fraction/decimal connections are useful priorities. These are product
recommendations based on the coverage above, not diagnoses from domain scores.

For each skill, proposed checks should vary the representation: solve an equation,
choose a matching story or drawing, construct a model, and explain or identify why
a strategy works. A correct numerical answer alone cannot establish every one of
those abilities. Keep performance on each kind of task distinguishable in reports,
and keep help and timing separate from correctness.

The existing advanced fraction and decimal operations should remain optional
challenges. They should not all become fourth-grade requirements: grade 4
specifically emphasizes fraction × whole-number work, like-denominator fraction
addition/subtraction, and decimal notation/comparison to hundredths.
